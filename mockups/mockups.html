Direction A: a plain menuA standard macOS menu and nothing else. Join is the first item, today’s and tomorrow’s meetings follow. It opens instantly and every row works from the keyboard.
Direction B: glanceWhat’s next and a Join button at the top. The ribbon below shows your whole day from 8 to 8, with a red line for now, so gaps and back-to-backs stand out.

Mini window, light

Mini window, dark

Add an event by typingType it the way you’d say it. Cue tints the parts it understood and turns them into chips you can click to change. The ribbon previews the slot on your day, with each person’s busy time striped in their colour. If someone’s busy, it offers the nearest free slots.
Pick a slot and createClick a suggested time or a chip to adjust it. ⌘↩ creates the event and sends the invites. A Google Meet link is added unless you turn it off.
Find a time with colleaguesAdd people and Cue lines up everyone’s day, one lane per person. Times when everyone is free are tinted green. This uses Google’s free/busy lookup, so it works for anyone at BrowserStack, even if they haven’t shared their calendar with you.
Click a meeting to see itClick any meeting in the list to open it in place: join link, your reply, who’s coming and the notes. Replying is one click.
Edit in placeClick the title, time or notes to edit them. Save sends the update to everyone invited. Delete asks first, and only appears on events you organise.
Hover a meeting to peekRest the pointer on a meeting for a moment and its details float beside the popover: time, call, room, who’s coming and the first lines of the notes. Move down the list and the card follows. Click still opens the full details.
The timeline tooEach block on the ribbon peeks the same way. The hovered block gets a ring so you can tell which one the card belongs to.
Meeting startingA banner drops in at the top of the screen when a meeting starts, even in full-screen apps. Press Return to join or Esc to dismiss. It closes itself after a minute.
